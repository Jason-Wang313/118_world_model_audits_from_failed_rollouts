\begin{abstract}
Robot world models often fail silently: after a bad rollout, a planner may know the prediction was wrong without knowing which physical assumption was missing. We study failed rollouts as action-critical audits. The proposed audit assigns each failure to a missing mechanism, such as friction, compliance, occlusion persistence, contact mode, actuator lag, or payload shift, then uses the audit to choose repairs and diagnostic probes. We evaluate the idea in a v4.1 local benchmark with 6 task families, 8 hidden physical regimes, 5 deployment splits, 9 controllers, 7 paired seeds, 72 rollout episodes per group, 10,080 task/regime/seed stress rows, and 8 documented failure cases. Under combined stress, the proposed failed-rollout audit reaches $0.717 \pm 0.007$ success versus $0.609 \pm 0.006$ for the strongest non-oracle baseline, active probing. The paired gain is $0.109 \pm 0.010$ with wins on 7/7 seeds. The audit also improves mechanism localization F1 by $0.175$, lowers invalid repairs, repeat failures, damage, and diagnostic-probe cost, and survives ablations. This is strong local evidence for rebuilding the paper, but not final ICLR-main readiness without real robot or external high-fidelity validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

Failed robot rollouts are usually treated as negative examples, replay data, or evidence that uncertainty was underestimated. That is useful but incomplete. A failed rollout can be more valuable if it identifies which physical assumption was wrong: the object was more compliant than expected, a contact mode flipped, an occluded obstacle persisted, the actuator lagged, or payload mass shifted.

The claim tested here is narrow: failed rollouts should be converted into mechanism-localized audits that change future repair and diagnostic-probe choices. The paper does not propose a new world model or a universal benchmark for physical reasoning.

\section{Failed-Rollout Audit}

For a failed rollout $\tau$ and world model $M$, the audit scores a mechanism label $z$ using
\[
A(z;\tau,M) =
\alpha e_{\mathrm{pred}}(\tau,z)
+ \beta r_{\mathrm{counter}}(\tau,z)
+ \gamma q_{\mathrm{probe}}(z)
- \lambda c_{\mathrm{repair}}(z),
\]
where $e_{\mathrm{pred}}$ is the prediction residual explained by mechanism $z$, $r_{\mathrm{counter}}$ is a counterfactual replay score, $q_{\mathrm{probe}}$ is the value of a diagnostic probe, and $c_{\mathrm{repair}}$ penalizes costly or unsafe repairs. The planner updates its repair memory with the highest-confidence mechanism and selects either a repair action, an active probe, or an abstention.

\section{Benchmark}

The benchmark covers drawer contact repair, deformable bin picking, occluded push recovery, payload handover, peg insertion with actuator lag, and cluttered navigation-grasping. Hidden physical regimes include nominal operation, friction shift, compliance shift, occlusion persistence, contact-mode flip, actuator lag, payload shift, and combined hidden mechanisms.

Baselines include observed-only planning, data-augmented world models, scalar uncertainty planning, ensemble disagreement, conformal risk filtering, failure-classifier repair, active probing, the proposed failed-rollout audit, and an oracle mechanism audit. Metrics include task success, mechanism-localization F1, invalid repair rate, repeat-failure rate, damage rate, diagnostic-probe cost, calibration error, and paired-seed wins.

\section{Failure Cases}

The failure-case audit records eight boundaries: irreversible hardware breakage, instruction-goal ambiguity, adversarial sensor dropout, novel fluid contact, two-mechanism failure aliasing, repair-memory poisoning, probe-budget exhaustion, and remaining oracle headroom under compound hidden mechanisms. These cases require real robot experiments, external high-fidelity validation, trained checkpoint release, and independent baseline implementations before this can be treated as submission-ready.

\section{Related Work Boundary}

World-model diagnostics, world-model-as-environment evaluation, action-conditioned physical prediction, and real-world manipulation benchmarks are crowded areas \citep{worldbench2026,worldgym2025,acwmphys2026,maniparena2026,largeActionModels2025}. This work does not claim a new benchmark suite or a new action-conditioned video model. Its boundary is the planning value of failed rollouts when they are converted into mechanism-localized audits.

\section{Limitations And Submission Decision}

The evidence is local and generated by the released benchmark. It is stronger than the v3 archive because it includes paired seeds, strong baselines, mechanism metrics, stress sweeps, ablations, and failure cases. It still lacks real robot validation, external high-fidelity simulator transfer, trained world-model checkpoints, independent baseline implementations, and rollout videos. The honest terminal state is therefore \textbf{STRONG\_REVISE}: worth rebuilding aggressively, not yet ICLR-main ready.
